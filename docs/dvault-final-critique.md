# Dvault final review

## Reference direction

[MetaMask](https://metamask.com/) puts a short proposition and a clear wallet action next to an image with a strong silhouette. [Ethereum](https://ethereum.org/) explains ownership at the top, then gives readers concrete paths into wallets and applications. Dvault needs that order too: identify the outcome, make the next action obvious, and explain the proof where the user sees it. The style target is Dvault's purple identity, a restrained dark canvas, hot scarlet interactions, three recognizable proof shapes, and a clear light equivalent.

## Audit of the previous build

| Surface | Why the experience felt unfinished | Final acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing | The short copy and coin render fit the viewport, but the display face read like handwriting and competed with the crypto identity. Stock spacing, a softly pink button and a single static asset made it look like a pitch page. | A heavier display face, smaller purpose-built UI face, deliberate word sequence, visually anchored character, and a clear entry action with a scarlet moving edge and focus/press behavior. |
| Return path | The app logo had a root URL but no visible text telling a user how to return. Reliance on the browser Back button mixed dashboard history with leaving the app. | Show “Back to landing” visibly on desktop and mobile. Use a normal link that can be focused and followed. |
| Dashboard | The first screen consisted of a large repeated mascot followed by two links. Clicking the rail swapped out the whole workspace. This hid library, service status, workflow, and visual material until another screen or nested guide toggle. | A single scroll-contained dashboard with Documents, live readiness, and Guide at the front. Visible desktop rail and mobile shortcut bar jump to sections. The file action should remain adjacent to the file area. |
| Mascot and icon quality | A 120px mark was enlarged to near 250px and rotated. This made an intentionally small mark appear blurred and crowded its card. CSS geometric pseudo icons had inconsistent stroke and meaning. | Keep the approved mark small, use a separate appropriately sized visual for larger illustration, and use authored consistent vector outlines. |
| Connectivity | A failed environment fetch was cached indefinitely. The UI could say disconnected without distinguishing a missing wallet from a reachable app with no permission, wrong chain, RPC failure, or absent deployment. A provider injected late could be missed. | Retry server configuration. Discover injected MetaMask, validate chain and registry independently, show actionable states inline, and keep wallet approval in MetaMask. |
| Setup | “Connect wallet” opened another button with the same label. Static progress strokes offered no perceivable confirmation of advancement, and coarse states obscured why a user was waiting. | One action that requests MetaMask access, truthful state text, a distinct key step, and animated active/progress indicators. |
| Upload | A tiny pulse was the only visible difference between encrypting, delivering bytes, remote pinning, and waiting for proof. The fetch API gave no transfer feedback. Success switched immediately to a different dialog. | Show Encrypting, Uploading, Stored, Waiting for signature, Confirming, and Verified as real transitions. Transfer percentage measures browser transfer only. Show a durable success receipt and offer a separate action to open the record. A failed proof must preserve a pinned payload, and a failed transfer must preserve its encrypted draft. |
| Model direction | Three similar discs rotated together with different engraved marks. Their similar profiles and materials made the encryption metaphor difficult to distinguish. | Distinct silhouettes and materials for encryption, storage, and verification. A separate, clearly labeled twelve-item proof carousel. Keep animation out of the way of upload and signing. |
| Sound and motion | Theme had no acknowledgment besides the immediate palette change, and there was no coordinated interaction vocabulary. Continuous layers could also compete with status text. | A short synthesized click on theme activation, with an optional mute control. Short land/reveal cues and isolated hover, press, and stage motion. Reduced motion follows OS preference. |
| Demo trust and performance | A local blockchain and wallet prompt are outside the control of visual animation. Slow pins, missing providers, and private phone certificates can look like app bugs without explanation. | Verify real network and storage boundaries, do not imply a physical device was tested via emulation, and keep large art off blocking UI paths. |

## Decisions after checking the live build

- **Landing typography.** The first rewrite wrapped twice at 1280px and pushed the action toward the bottom. Two shorter lines keep the heavy display face clear of the guardian. The body uses Geist at smaller sizes. The entry action remains at the left on desktop and spans the content width on a phone. Its arrow has a circular trailing target, and the moving border and fill highlights are separate.
- **Navigation and placement.** A labeled return link sits beside the logo. The rail jumps to sections rather than removing others. The initial mobile grid gave Documents a zero-height track and laid Guide on top of it. Explicit max-content rows fixed the overlap. At 360×640, the Overview, Documents, and Guide panel boundaries were 438, 450–690, and 702px before internal scrolling, and the navigation was anchored at 640px. There was no horizontal overflow.
- **Visual identity.** The purple mascot remains small in the logo and document empty state. The large area uses a custom shield glyph. The landing guardian is an original fox drawn as SVG and animated on a depth-displaced WebGL surface, with the authored SVG as its reduced motion and graphics fallback. It is not a rigged Meshy animal.
- **WebGL versus Meshy.** I loaded one existing Meshy verification seal in a visual comparison. The model rendered but its rim and round silhouette resembled the previous set. The shipped WebGL lock has a rounded square rim and ruby light; storage has a bevelled diamond rim and violet light; the seal is circular with mint chrome. A lift and drop separates the marks during the focus change. The twelve-item collection is a separate keyboard and touch navigable row with distinct authored glyphs and three-dimensional swaps. It does not require twelve full GLBs. No new Meshy credits were spent. The comparison remains available at `/v2/app/?mesh=verify#guide` for review.
- **Theme.** The same controls and section order work in both themes. The theme button plays a short synthesized sound only after a click; the sound control mutes it. Desktop and compact phone navigation, theme, entry/return, and fallback labels were checked in the browser. GPU work pauses when hidden and CSS effects reduce on request. I did not measure frame rate on physical phones.

## Verified evidence

- Browser at 1280×720: fixed dashboard frame, horizontally contained content pane, registry online on chain 31337, 12 selectable marks, custom renderer, explicit Back to landing navigation. The landing used two title lines and fit the viewport.
- Browser at 360×640: landing CTA bottom at 596px and no outer scrolling; light-mode dashboard kept the bottom bar fixed and the stacked panels separated.
- Disposable browser fixture: connected wallet, generated and acknowledged a recovery key, unlocked, selected a synthetic file, and delivered an encrypted envelope. Observed IPFS waiting, wallet approval, confirmation, and the success receipt as separate visible states. The receipt opened Version 1. The fixture simulated storage and blockchain responses; it did not use a personal wallet.
- Separate live verification: two synthetic encrypted revisions through real Pinata, IPFS retrieval, and the active Hardhat registry. The isolated crypto and contract regression also passed. These checks cover content integrity and signed version history, not physical MetaMask Mobile approval or PWA installation.
